### Solving the "Noisy Neighbor": How Sharded Architecture Protects Multi-Tenant Platforms
**Source:** Google Cloud (General)

> **Category:** [GCP_K8S_CORE]
> **Summary:** The article discusses the "noisy neighbor" problem in multi-tenant platforms, where a single tenant's massive data burst or failing database instance can bring down the entire platform. It proposes a sharded hub-and-spoke architecture as a solution, where data is decoupled into a "hub" for routing and "spokes" for isolated execution. The hub uses Dataflow to route data to isolated buffers, and the spokes use Pub/Sub topics as durable shock absorbers to prevent backpressure. The article highlights the benefits of this architecture, including fault tolerance, reduced blast radius, independent scaling, and safer deployments.
> **Impact:** The sharded hub-and-spoke architecture can significantly improve the resilience and scalability of multi-tenant platforms, allowing for independent scaling and safer deployments. By isolating tenants and workloads, the architecture reduces the risk of a single "noisy neighbor" bringing down the entire platform, ensuring stricter SLAs and improved overall performance.

### Metric cardinality limits in OpenTelemetry: a practical guide
**Source:** OpenTelemetry Blog

> **Category:** [OPS_STACK]
> **Summary:** OpenTelemetry metrics have a cardinality limit to prevent unbounded memory growth due to unique attribute combinations. However, when this limit is exceeded, the total value remains correct, but queries that filter or group by attributes can undercount, affecting dashboards, SLOs, and alerts.
> **Impact:** This can have significant operational impact, as undercounting can lead to incorrect decisions and potentially cause issues with service reliability and performance monitoring, highlighting the need for careful consideration of metric cardinality limits in OpenTelemetry implementations.
